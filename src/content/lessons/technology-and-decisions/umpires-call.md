## The short answer

Ball-tracking can't measure with perfect precision, so the review system builds in a margin. If the tracking shows the ball only **just** hitting the zone that matters — between 1% and 50% of the ball inside it — the on-field umpire's original decision stands. That margin is **umpire's call**.

## The three questions in an LBW review

1. **Where did it pitch?** There is **no** umpire's call here. If more than 50% of the ball pitched outside the line of leg stump, it counts as outside. Exactly 50/50 goes to the bowling side.
2. **Where did it hit the batter?** The **impact zone** is the space between the outer edges of off and leg stump, from the ground upward. If 1–50% of the ball is in it, it's umpire's call.
3. **Was it hitting the stumps?** The **wicket zone** runs from the base of the stumps to the top of the bails, across the outer edges of off and leg stump. If 1–50% of the ball hits it, it's umpire's call. Before 2021 the zone stopped at the bottom of the bails.

## Distance rules

Predictions are less reliable when the ball has to be projected a long way, or when there is little tracked path after the bounce. The review conditions therefore also return umpire's call when:

- the point of impact is **3 metres or more** from the stumps; or
- the batter is more than **2.5 m** from the stumps and the ball pitched within **40 cm** of hitting the pad.

## Why it exists

Umpire's call is a way of saying: *if the technology can't be confident the umpire was wrong, don't overturn the umpire*. Critics argue the same ball can then be both out and not out depending on the on-field call. The ICC reviewed the system in 2021 and kept it.

## Common misconceptions

- *"Umpire's call means the technology is unsure if it hit."* It means the tracked ball is close enough to the edge of the zone that a small error could change the answer.
- *"A team loses a review for umpire's call."* Under current playing conditions, a review that returns umpire's call is retained.

Since 2017, a team's review is not lost when the result is umpire's call. Teams still lose a review when the decision is clearly upheld. Exact numbers of reviews per innings depend on the format's playing conditions.
