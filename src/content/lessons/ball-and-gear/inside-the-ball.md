## The short answer

A cricket ball is built in layers: a **cork core**, wound tightly with **string**, inside a **leather case** that is stitched together with a raised **seam**. Law 4 fixes its weight and size. Over an innings, impacts compress the inside and scuff the outside — which is why an old ball behaves so differently from a new one.

## What the Laws say

| Ball | Weight | Circumference |
|---|---|---|
| Men's (and boys 13+) | 5.5–5.75 oz (155.9–163 g) | 8.81–9 in (22.4–22.9 cm) |
| Women's (and girls 13+) | 4.94–5.31 oz (140–151 g) | 8.25–8.88 in (21.0–22.5 cm) |

Junior balls are smaller and lighter again.

## Construction

- **Core.** Cork, often in layers, wound with string under tension.
- **Case.** Four pieces of leather in top-quality balls (two in cheaper ones). Pairs are stitched internally to form two halves — the line where they meet is the **quarter seam**.
- **Main seam.** The two halves are joined with a raised seam around the equator, typically six rows of stitching.
- **Finish.** The leather is dyed and lacquered. Red is traditional; white is used for most limited-overs cricket; pink for day-night Tests, because it is easier to see under lights.

## How it wears

- **Softening.** Repeated impacts compress the core and windings. A softer ball bounces lower and comes off the bat slower.
- **Scuffing.** Abrasive pitches and outfields roughen the leather — which, combined with shining one side, sets up reverse swing.
- **Seam flattening.** The seam gets less prominent, so the ball seams less off the pitch.

## Common misconceptions

- *"All balls are the same."* Within the Laws' limits, manufacturers make balls that behave noticeably differently.
- *"The quarter seam doesn't matter."* Bowlers sometimes use it deliberately for different movement.

<summary>Go deeper: why the new ball is so precious</summary>

A new ball is at its hardest, shiniest and with the most prominent seam. That means maximum bounce, the clearest contrast for swing and the most bite for seam movement. Test teams can take a new ball after 80 overs, and much of the game's strategy revolves around using it well.
