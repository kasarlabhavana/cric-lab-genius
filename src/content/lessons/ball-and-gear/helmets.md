## The short answer

A ball hitting a helmet has to transfer its momentum somehow. The helmet's job is to make that happen **over a larger area** and **over a longer time**, which lowers the peak force on the head. International cricket requires helmets certified to **BS 7928:2013**, a standard that tests impact protection and checks that the ball cannot get through the face guard.

## The physics

Newton's second law, in impulse form, says force × time = change in momentum. A ball's momentum doesn't change much whatever hits it, so:

- **Longer contact time → lower force.** The foam liner crushes and stretches out the impact.
- **Larger area → lower pressure.** The hard shell spreads the load so no single point takes it all.

## What the standard tests

BS 7928:2013 includes:

- **Impact tests** with cricket balls fired at the helmet at set speeds and positions.
- **Face-guard tests** to check that the grille doesn't deform into the face.
- **Penetration tests** to check that the ball can't pass between the peak and the grille.

Since **February 2017**, the ICC has enforced the rule that batters in men's and women's internationals who choose to wear a helmet must wear one certified to this standard. Helmets aren't compulsory under those regulations, but almost all international batters wear one.

## On the field

- **Neck protection.** Many players now wear clip-on neck guards to protect the area below the helmet, after high-profile injuries.
- **Concussion substitutes.** International cricket allows like-for-like replacements for players diagnosed with concussion, encouraging teams to remove injured players rather than play on.

## Common misconceptions

- *"A helmet makes the batter safe."* It reduces risk; it doesn't remove it. Impacts can still cause concussion.
- *"A heavier helmet protects better."* Protection depends on design and materials, not weight.

<details>
<summary>Go deeper: why a hard shell and a soft liner</summary>

The shell stops penetration and spreads the load; the liner absorbs energy by crushing. Too soft a liner bottoms out on hard impacts; too stiff and it doesn't extend the stopping time enough. Helmet design is a balance between the two.
