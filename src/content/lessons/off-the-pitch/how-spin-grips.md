## The short answer

A spinning ball's surface is moving relative to the pitch when it lands. If the surface grips, friction pushes the ball in the direction of that movement and it **turns**. The drier, rougher and more worn the pitch, the more it grips. That is why spinners usually get more help as a match goes on.

## How it works

At the moment of contact, the bottom of a spinning ball is moving sideways (for side-spin) or forwards/backwards (for top-spin or back-spin) relative to the ground. Friction opposes that relative motion:

- **Side-spin** gives a sideways push — **turn**.
- **Top-spin** — the ball's underside moving backwards against the ground — is converted into extra forward and upward speed, so the ball **kicks** and bounces higher.
- **Back-spin** makes the ball **skid** on lower and quicker.

Most spin deliveries mix these, and spinners vary the mix with wrist and finger position at release.

Two things decide how much turn is possible: the **revs** on the ball and the **grip** of the surface. A dusty, dry pitch grips more. A damp or polished surface lets the ball skid on.

## On the field

- **Footmarks.** Bowlers' follow-throughs rough up areas outside the stumps. Spinners target them because the ball grips more and bounces unevenly there.
- **Slower through the air on a turner.** On a pitch that grips, spinners often bowl a touch slower to give the ball more time to bite.
- **The ball that doesn't turn.** On a turning pitch, the delivery that skids straight on is as dangerous as the one that rips, because the batter is playing for turn.

## Common misconceptions

- *"Turn comes from the air."* Drift comes from the air. Turn comes from the pitch.
- *"More revs always means more turn."* Revs give the potential; the surface decides how much of it becomes turn.

<details>
<summary>Go deeper: why bounce varies on turning pitches</summary>

On a worn pitch, the surface is no longer uniform. Some spots are hard and smooth, others crumbly. A ball landing on a crumbling patch loses more energy and keeps low; one landing on a firm patch with top-spin can climb. That variation, more than raw turn, is often what makes a turning pitch hard to bat on.
