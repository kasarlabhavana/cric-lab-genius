## The short answer

The Laws say a bowler must not **straighten** the elbow during the delivery swing — that would be a throw. But when researchers measured bowling actions with motion capture, they found that almost every bowler extends the elbow a little, even those whose actions looked perfectly legal. The ICC therefore allows up to **15°** of elbow extension, measured in a laboratory, for every bowler.

## How it works

- **Law 21** defines a fair delivery: once the bowling arm reaches shoulder level in the delivery swing, the elbow must not be straightened, partially or fully, before the ball is released. Bending the wrist is allowed.
- **The measurement.** In ICC testing, markers are placed on the arm and high-speed cameras record many deliveries. Software calculates how much the elbow angle changes between the arm reaching horizontal and the moment of release.
- **The threshold.** Extension up to 15° is permitted. The figure applies to all bowlers from 1 March 2005, replacing earlier, different limits for spinners and pace bowlers. The ICC described 15° as roughly the point at which straightening becomes visible to the naked eye.

## Why it matters

- **Appearance misleads.** A bowler with a naturally bent or hyper-extending arm can look suspicious while staying within the limit. That is why the decision rests on laboratory measurement rather than an umpire's view.
- **Some variations are higher-risk.** Deliveries that need extra wrist and forearm effort — such as some off-spinners' variations — have been the focus of many reports.
- **Remodelling.** Bowlers reported and found above the limit can remodel their action and be re-tested.

## Common misconceptions

- *"A bent arm means a throw."* A bent arm that stays bent is legal. The question is whether the elbow straightens.
- *"15° is a lot."* It was chosen as roughly the smallest straightening the naked eye can detect at bowling speed — below that, umpires could not reliably call it.

<details>
<summary>Go deeper: hyperextension and carrying angle</summary>

Some people's elbows naturally extend past straight (hyperextension), and many have a natural carrying angle where the forearm angles away from the upper arm. Both can make an action look odd without any illegal straightening. Measuring the change in angle, rather than the angle itself, is the point of the test.
